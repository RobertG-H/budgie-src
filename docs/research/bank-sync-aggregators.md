# Research: bank-sync aggregators for Canadian banks

Research for [#37](https://github.com/RobertG-H/budgie-src/issues/37), part of the map [#33](https://github.com/RobertG-H/budgie-src/issues/33).
Gathered 2026-09-30 from each aggregator's own API docs, OpenAPI specs and help centre, plus the Government of Canada for open-banking status.

**Question:** what shape do accounts and transactions take in the bank-sync aggregators that cover Canadian banks, so that a future bank sync fits the same Account and Bank transaction tables as CSV import?

## Summary

- **Every aggregator gives a stable, opaque string (or integer) ID per account and per transaction, scoped to one connection.** None of them promise the ID survives a disconnect and reconnect: a new connection gets new account and transaction IDs (Plaid, Flinks).
- **Pending transactions don't keep their ID when they post, in general.** Plaid removes the pending one and adds a new posted one that points back through `pending_transaction_id`. MX keeps the ID when it can match them, but says delete-and-replace is "the most common scenario". Yodlee creates a new ID by default. Flinks never returns pending transactions at all. Pending ones can also vanish without ever posting (authorization holds).
- **Only Plaid has a true delta feed** (`/transactions/sync`: `added`, `modified`, `removed` against a cursor). MX has webhooks with `created`/`updated`/`deleted` and a `from_updated_at` filter. Flinks, Finicity and SimpleFIN hand back a window of transactions and leave diffing to you.
- **Sign conventions disagree.** Plaid: positive = money out. Finicity and SimpleFIN: positive = money in. MX and Yodlee: unsigned amount plus a `CREDIT`/`DEBIT` type. Flinks: separate `Debit` and `Credit` columns.
- **One connection = one institution login**, which holds several accounts: Plaid's Item, MX's member, Flinks' LoginId, Finicity's institution login. A user links several institutions by making several connections.
- **CIBC and the big five:** Plaid and Flinks both list CIBC, RBC, TD, BMO and Scotiabank. MX claims US and Canada deposit coverage without a per-bank list. Mastercard's (Finicity's) published US API marks every endpoint US-only. SimpleFIN's Canadian coverage couldn't be confirmed from its docs.
- **Hobby-scale access:** Plaid's free Trial plan (US/Canada teams created on or after 2026-04-15, 10 Production Items) is the only realistic free route to real Canadian bank data. SimpleFIN Bridge is US$15/year but Canadian coverage is unverified. Flinks starts at $500/month. MX, Yodlee and Finicity go through sales for production.
- **Canadian open banking isn't live yet.** Consumer-driven banking regulations were pre-published on 2026-06-26 and phase in from accreditation over a year after final publication. Screen scraping stays legal until the framework is "fully operational". Today every aggregator reaches Canadian banks by credentials; Plaid says "OAuth connections are not currently used by financial institutions in Canada."

## Plaid

**Shape.** An **Item** is "a login at a financial institution. A single end-user of your application might have accounts at different financial institutions, which means they would have multiple different Items." An Item holds several **accounts**. "Two Items created for the same set of credentials at the same institution will be considered different and not share the same `item_id`." ([Glossary](https://plaid.com/docs/quickstart/glossary/))

**Account ID.** `account_id` "will not change unless Plaid can't reconcile the account with the data returned by the financial institution". It can change when the account's name changes, or when the Item is deleted and the user relinks with the same credentials later. `persistent_account_id`, which survives across Items, exists only for a few US banks that use tokenized account numbers. Accounts also carry `type`, `subtype`, `mask` (last 2–4 characters, not guaranteed unique within an Item), `name`, `official_name` and `balances`. ([Accounts API](https://plaid.com/docs/api/accounts/))

**Sync.** `/transactions/sync` takes a cursor and returns `added`, `modified` and `removed` (transaction IDs only), ordered by last-modified time, with `has_more` and `next_cursor`. Omitting the cursor returns the full history. If a page fails with `TRANSACTIONS_SYNC_MUTATION_DURING_PAGINATION`, restart from the original cursor. Cursors stay valid for at least a year. ([Transactions API](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/)) The `SYNC_UPDATES_AVAILABLE` webhook fires "whenever any change has happened to the Item's transactions". Plaid checks for new transactions "one to four times per day", and history goes back up to 24 months. ([Transactions webhooks](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/webhooks/))

**Pending vs posted.** When a transaction posts, sync puts "the pending transaction's `transaction_id` in the `removed` field" and adds a new posted transaction whose `pending_transaction_id` points to the removed one. Pending-to-posted "typically takes about one to five business days… up to fourteen days in rare situations". "Their name and amount may change" between the two, a tip for example. Some institutions give no pending data or no match, so `pending_transaction_id` is `null`. A pending transaction "may not convert to a posted transaction at all and will simply disappear", such as an authorization hold. "A posted transaction cannot necessarily be considered immutable." ([Transactions data](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/transactions-data/))

**Fields.** `transaction_id` (case-sensitive string), `account_id`, `amount` ("Positive values when money moves out of the account; negative values when money moves in"), `iso_currency_code`/`unofficial_currency_code`, `date` (posted date, or the transaction date when pending), `authorized_date`, `pending`, `pending_transaction_id`, `name` (raw description), `merchant_name` (enriched, nullable), `personal_finance_category` (primary and detailed categories with a confidence level) and `payment_channel`. ([Transactions API](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/))

**Canada.** Plaid lists CIBC with Assets, Auth, Balance and Transactions, for depository, credit, loan and investment accounts ([CIBC](https://plaid.com/institutions/cibc/)). Plaid's Canada launch post, dated 2018-05-22 and so old, names RBC, Scotiabank, TD, BMO, CIBC and Tangerine ([Plaid in Canada](https://plaid.com/blog/plaid-in-canada/)). For current per-bank product support, use `/institutions/get` or the Dashboard ([Institutions](https://plaid.com/docs/institutions/)). Link needs `CA` in `country_codes`. "OAuth connections are not currently used by financial institutions in Canada" ([OAuth guide](https://plaid.com/docs/link/oauth/)), so Canadian Items are credential-based and break when the user changes their password.

**Cost and access.** Transactions is billed as a subscription: "an Item will incur a monthly subscription fee as long as a valid `access_token` exists for the Item", except for `/transactions/refresh`, which is billed per request. "Free Trial plans are available to new Plaid teams (US/Canada only) created on or after April 15, 2026". "You can create 10 Production Items on a Trial plan", and removing an Item doesn't free its slot. Pay-as-you-go has no minimum and is "most appropriate for hobbyist use". Sandbox is always free. ([Billing](https://plaid.com/docs/account/billing/)) The per-Item price for Transactions isn't published on the pricing page ([Pricing](https://plaid.com/pricing/)).

## Flinks (Canadian)

**Shape.** Flinks Connect returns a non-expiring **LoginId** per institution login. `/GetAccountsDetail`, called with a `RequestId` from `/Authorize`, returns the login's accounts, each with its transactions. It often answers `202` at first, and you poll `/GetAccountsDetailAsync`. Options include `DaysOfTransactions` (`Days90` or `Days365`) and `AccountsFilter`. ([GetAccountsDetail](https://docs.flinks.com/api/connect/endpoints/account-linking/get-accounts-detail))

**Fields.** Accounts: `Id`, `AccountNumber`, `LastFourDigits`, `TransitNumber`, `InstitutionNumber`, `Title`, `Balance` (`Available`, `Current`, `Limit`), `Category`, `Type`, `Currency` and `AccountType` (Personal or Business). Transactions: `Id` ("Flinks' transaction ID"), `Date`, `Code`, `Description` ("as presented by the financial institution"), `Debit`, `Credit` and `Balance` ("Account balance after the transaction"). ([Data types](https://help.flinks.com/support/solutions/articles/43000705311-data-types-in-getaccountsdetail)) The sign lives in which column is filled: debit is money out and credit is money in. There's no merchant or category in the core payload; that's the separate Flinks Enrich product.

**IDs.** LoginId, AccountId and TransactionId are kept "Forever". The LoginId changes if the username changes or if the user deletes the connection and reconnects, and a new LoginId brings new AccountIds and TransactionIds. AccountId and TransactionId are random and "unique across the board". ([IDs](https://help.flinks.com/support/solutions/articles/43000681817-for-how-long-flinks-holds-data-and-ids-)) A password change keeps the LoginId but needs fresh credentials ([LoginId](https://help.flinks.com/support/solutions/articles/43000436115-what-happen-to-the-loginid-if-the-user-changes-his-password-)).

**Pending.** "The transaction payload exclusively contains transactions that have been fully processed and posted to the account", and there's no status field. Pending totals show up only as `Available` minus `Current` balance. ([Posted vs pending](https://help.flinks.com/support/solutions/articles/43000771380-post-vs-pending-transactions-api-behaviour))

**Changes.** There's no delta or removal feed. A Nightly Refresh, 4–8 a.m. UTC, keeps cached data current for logins with `ScheduleRefresh` set, and you re-read the full list and diff by `Id`. ([Nightly refreshes](https://help.flinks.com/support/solutions/articles/43000637682-nightly-refreshes))

**Canada.** Flinks lists BMO, CIBC, Coast Capital, Desjardins, EQ Bank, Laurentienne, Meridian, National, RBC/HSBC, Scotia, Simplii, Tangerine and TD ([Supported institutions](https://docs.flinks.com/guides/connect/supported-institutions)). It has the deepest Canadian list of the options here.

**Cost and access.** There's no self-serve trial, but the sandbox is free. Flinks Connect starts at $500/month for 200 unique connections, on a one-year term ([Pricing](https://www.flinks.com/pricing)). That's not viable for a hobby app.

## MX

**Shape.** A **user** is your end user, and a **member** is "the relationship between a user and an institution". A user can have several members, and each member has accounts. To fetch data you call the member's aggregate endpoint, poll its status, then list accounts and transactions by account, member or user. ([Fetch data guide](https://docs.mx.com/other/legacy-connectivity-guides/fetch-account-transaction-data-api/))

**Fields**, from MX's published OpenAPI spec ([mxenabled/openapi `v20250224.yml`](https://github.com/mxenabled/openapi/blob/master/openapi/v20250224.yml), `TransactionResponse`): `guid` ("Defined by MX", e.g. `TRN-…`), `id` (partner-defined), `account_guid`, `member_guid`, `amount` (unsigned in the examples), `type` (`CREDIT` or `DEBIT`), `status` (`POSTED` or `PENDING`), `date`, `transacted_at`, `posted_at`, `description`, `original_description` ("as provided by our data feed"), `merchant_guid`, `category`, `top_level_category`, `currency_code`, flags such as `is_income`, `is_expense` and `is_fee`, and `updated_at`. List endpoints take `from_updated_at` and `to_updated_at`, as Unix timestamps.

**Pending vs posted**, quoting the spec's `status` description: "Many institutions do not provide data for pending transactions; transactions from those accounts always have a status of `POSTED`. When we do receive data for pending transactions, a single transaction may be updated from `PENDING` to `POSTED` and keep the same `guid`… If a single transaction can't be updated, the `PENDING` transaction will often be deleted and replaced with a new `POSTED` transaction (with a new `guid`)… this is the most common scenario when pending data is available… All `PENDING` transactions are deleted after 14 days as a failsafe."

**Changes.** Transaction webhooks carry an `action` of `created`, `updated` or `deleted` ([Transactions webhook](https://docs.mx.com/resources/webhooks/transactions.md)).

**Canada and access.** MX claims "the most comprehensive coverage for demand deposit accounts across U.S. and Canada" but publishes no per-bank list ([Account aggregation](https://www.mx.com/products/account-aggregation/)). A development environment is available, and production access goes through MX sales ([API access](https://www.mx.com/api-access/)). CIBC coverage is unverified.

## Finicity (Mastercard Open Finance)

**Fields**, from Mastercard's published OpenAPI spec ([Mastercard/open-banking-us-openapi `openbanking-us.yaml`](https://github.com/Mastercard/open-banking-us-openapi), `Transaction`): `id` (int64), `uniqueTransactionId` ("globally unique… derived from accountId and transactionId"), `accountId`, `customerId`, `amount` ("Transactions for deposits are positive values, withdrawals and debits are negative values"), `status` ("active", "pending" or "shadow"), `description` ("often known as the payee"), `memo`, `transactionDate`, `postedDate`, `createdDate`, `categorization` (`normalizedPayeeName`, `category`, …) and `runningBalanceAmount`. Accounts are grouped under an `institutionLoginId`.

**Pending and changes.** `GET /aggregation/v3/customers/{customerId}/transactions` returns a `fromDate`–`toDate` window, and `includePending` defaults to `false`. There's no delta feed. Mastercard's developer docs, as indexed by search because the page renders in JavaScript, say that when a transaction posts "the pending transaction may disappear in the data, and a new transaction with status 'active' may appear". A "shadow" transaction is one that was "found in an earlier aggregation… but [is] not found in the institution's current data source". ([Understanding transaction data](https://developer.mastercard.com/open-finance-us/documentation/products/manage/transaction-data/understanding-transaction-data/)) Accounts refresh automatically "once per day", and apps "are not permitted to automate calls to the Refresh services".

**Canada.** Every one of the 142 region-tagged endpoints in the published US spec is marked US-only (🇺🇸), and none is marked for Canada. I found no Canadian Open Finance API on developer.mastercard.com. Treat Finicity as not an option for Canadian banks.

## Others

- **Yodlee (Envestnet).** Its transaction model has `id` ("The combination of the id and account container is unique"), `amount` and `baseType` (`CREDIT` or `DEBIT`), `status` (`POSTED`, `PENDING`, `SCHEDULED`, …), `transactionDate`, `postDate`, original and simple `description`, `merchant` and `category` ([Transactions resource](https://developer.yodlee.com/Yodlee_API/docs/v1_1/Data_Model/Resource_Transactions)). "The current behavior of the platform creates unique transaction IDs whenever a transaction transitions from Pending to Posted", unless a reconciliation config key is enabled ([May 2025 release notes](https://developer.yodlee.com/resources/yodlee/may-2025-release-notes)). Its docs mention Canadian provider sites, and access is enterprise sales.
- **SimpleFIN Bridge.** The protocol is a plain read-only `GET /accounts?start-date=&end-date=&pending=1`. Transaction `id` "may never [be reused] within an account". `amount` is signed ("Positive numbers indicate money being deposited"), and there are `posted` (0 when pending), `transacted_at`, `description`, `payee`, `memo` and `pending`. There's no delta or removal mechanism ([Protocol](https://www.simplefin.org/protocol.html)). The docs don't say whether a pending ID survives posting. Pricing is US$1.50/month or US$15/year for up to 25 institutions. It allows about 24 requests a day, gives daily updates and caps each request at 90 days ([Bridge](https://beta-bridge.simplefin.org/), [Developer guide](https://beta-bridge.simplefin.org/info/developers)). The Bridge says it supports "US/Canadian versions of internationally established institutions", but its institution search is interactive, so **CIBC coverage couldn't be confirmed**. It's worth checking by hand, because it's the only hobby-priced option.

## Canadian open banking status

- The Budget 2025 framework mandates participation "for banks based on a threshold for retail volume". Phase 1 is read access to deposit, payment, investment and lending data. Phase 2, write access, comes after "policy work over the next 12 to 18 months". Participants need accreditation, including national-security screening, and individual developers aren't addressed. "The prohibition of screen scraping will come into force once the framework is fully operational." ([Budget 2025 framework](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/financial-sector-policy/open-banking-implementation/budget-2025-canadas-framework-for-consumer-driven-banking.html))
- On 2026-06-26 the proposed Consumer-Driven Banking Regulations were pre-published for a 60-day comment period. They'll "come into force in a staggered approach beginning with accreditation, followed by requirements related to common rules and assessment fees within one year of final publication". About nine million Canadians still share data by screen scraping. ([Pre-publication news release](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/06/government-pre-publishes-regulations-to-prevent-fraud-and-facilitate-the-next-phase-of-consumer-driven-banking.html))
- So for the next year or more, Canadian bank sync means credential-based aggregation through an accredited-in-waiting aggregator. A hobby app can't become an accredited party itself.

## Implications for Budgie's model

1. **Bank transaction keeps an external ID scoped to its Account**, with a unique index on `(account_id, external_id)` where the ID is non-null. CSV rows have no ID from the bank, so they need their own dedupe key, such as a hash of date, amount and description plus an occurrence counter. That means the external ID column is nullable or source-specific, not the only identity.
2. **Account keeps an external account ID and a connection reference** (provider plus Item, member or LoginId). A reconnect can mint new account and transaction IDs (Plaid, Flinks), so re-linking must let the user map the new external account onto the existing Budgie Account, and dedupe must fall back to content matching around the seam.
3. **Model pending as a separate Bank transaction that can be superseded or removed**, not as a mutable status on one row. The common case in Plaid, MX and Yodlee is "pending removed, new posted row added", with an optional back-pointer (Plaid's `pending_transaction_id`), and pending rows can simply disappear. Budgie should either not file pending transactions at all, which is the simplest option and matches Flinks and CSV, or allow a filed pending transaction to be re-pointed to its posted successor. Filing only posted transactions keeps budget records stable.
4. **Bank transactions must support "modified" and "removed" after import.** Plaid says posted transactions aren't immutable. Store the provider's latest raw values and a `removed_at` or tombstone rather than hard-deleting, so a Deposit, Spent or Refund that was filed from it can be flagged for review instead of silently orphaned.
5. **Normalize amount sign once, at the importer boundary.** Store one signed amount with a fixed convention, such as positive = money into the account, and keep the provider's raw fields separately. The adapters differ: Plaid flips the sign, Flinks and MX need a column or type mapped to a sign, and Finicity and SimpleFIN pass through. The CSV importer faces the same problem.
6. **Keep two dates**, the transacted or authorized date and the posted date, both nullable. Plaid, MX, Finicity and SimpleFIN all distinguish them, and Plaid recommends `authorized_date` for when money actually moved.
7. **Description is raw, and payee and category are optional provider hints.** Store the raw description plus a nullable merchant or payee name. Treat provider categories as suggestions at most, because Budgie's Envelopes are the user's own categories.
8. **Sync state lives on the connection, not the Account.** Store Plaid's cursor or MX's last `updated_at` per connection. Flinks, Finicity and SimpleFIN need a date-window re-read and a diff by external ID, so the importer should be idempotent on `(account, external_id)` whatever the provider.
9. **Currency per Account.** Every provider reports an ISO currency per account or transaction. Budgie has one currency per Budget, so an Account in another currency should be refused or flagged at link time.
10. **Provider choice, if bank sync happens:** Plaid's Trial, with 10 Items and CIBC covered, is the only confirmed free path. Check SimpleFIN's CIBC coverage by hand before ruling it in. CSV import stays the primary path, because Canadian bank sync is credential-based until open banking lands.
